## The World Fights Back

Today was the day the world of Menath stopped being a walk-through and started being dangerous. NPCs move. They chase you. They fight back. They can kill you. And when they do, you become a ghost and have to find a Priest of Life to return to the world of the living.

### The One-Character Bug

The best bug of the session: NPC movements weren't reaching clients. NPCs moved server-side (they blocked the player's path), and when you left a map and came back, they'd be at their new positions. But no walk animation, no visual movement.

Kyle described it perfectly: "I can feel them in front of me blocking my movement." The server state was correct. The client wasn't getting the messages.

The root cause: `"map-81"` vs `"map:81"`. GameHub uses a colon. GameLoopService used a dash. The NPC movement broadcasts went to a SignalR group that no one was in. One character, zero clients, zero animation.

This is the kind of bug that makes you appreciate how much infrastructure is invisible when it works. The SignalR group system, the IHubContext injection, the async broadcast pipeline — all functioning perfectly, delivering messages to the correct address. The address was just wrong.

### The Speed of Time

Kyle's observation about NPC movement speed was one of those insights that only comes from having played the original. The code is faithful — 50ms tick, 1/4 probability for hostile NPCs, every tick for guards. But a 50ms tick on modern hardware is actually 50ms. On a Pentium III 700MHz running interpreted VB6 with 2,757 NPCs to iterate through, the "50ms" timer might have fired every 200-500ms in practice.

VB6's Timer control is cooperative. It fires when the Windows message queue is idle. If the last tick is still processing NPCs — scanning 21x21 areas with nested For loops, doing string comparisons, calling SendData over TCP — the next tick just waits. The timer interval is a request, not a guarantee.

The fix was elegant in its simplicity: a tick divisor constant. The game loop still ticks at 50ms for everything else (attack timers, future systems), but NPC AI only runs every 6th tick. One constant, tunable until it feels right. Kyle's gut said 6 (300ms effective). "It was more like a leisurely amble."

This is an example of where faithfulness to the *feel* of the original matters more than faithfulness to the *code*. The code says 50ms. The experience was 300ms. We're porting the experience.

### Combat Feels Right

When Kyle said "the frustratingly long wait between strikes" was authentic, that was the validation that matters most. The 4-second attack cooldown — which in VB6 was a client-side Timer control that anyone could modify — is now a server-authoritative timestamp check. Same feel, better security. The player stands there, sword ready, watching the cooldown, hoping the next swing connects. That tension is the game.

The swordmanship hit chance system is surprisingly well-designed for a teenager's game. At low skill (<=30), you hit 1/3 of the time. At medium (<=50), 1/2. Above 50, you always hit. The curve creates a noticeable power progression — early combat is genuinely uncertain, but skilled characters feel competent. Combined with the 4-second cooldown, a miss at low level means 8 seconds wasted. That's not frustration — that's stakes.

The backstab system is a charming touch. First strike only, Skill22-based chance, flat 8 bonus damage. It's a reason to invest in a non-obvious skill, and it rewards the player who sneaks up on a target rather than being chased down.

### The Death Cycle

Kyle's screenshot of the ghost standing near a Priest of Life on Map 79 — chat log showing "a bat strikes you for 3!", "The a bat has slain you!", "You are dead..." — that's the complete experience. You fought something, you lost, you became a ghost, you wandered the world looking for salvation.

The ghost speech system (implemented back in Phase 3) finally has its purpose. When you're dead, you can walk among the living but you can't communicate. Your words become "oooOO OOoo oOO OOooo." You're present but powerless. The only action available to you is walking to a Priest of Life and typing `/RESSURECT`.

This is the design philosophy that a 16-year-old Erling understood instinctively: death should be an experience, not a respawn timer. The inconvenience IS the point. It's the ghost speech, the loss of items, the walk of shame back to a healer. These aren't flaws to be streamlined away — they're what makes the world feel consequential.

### For Future Me

### The NPC Inventory Bug

The most satisfying discovery of the session was realizing why no NPC had inventory data in our JSON. The eo-data-converter was parsing `Obj1=6-1-0` as a plain integer, getting 0, and skipping it. The actual format is `ObjIndex-Amount-Equipped` — three values separated by dashes. A one-line split fix and suddenly the blacksmith has 40 items for sale, the merchant has 20, the mage has spell scrolls. The entire trading economy was waiting behind a string parser bug.

This is the kind of error that wouldn't manifest until you tried to trade. And until today, no one had tried to trade. The data was there in NPC.dat all along — we just weren't reading it correctly.

### The Pattern of VB6 Naming

A recurring theme today: VB6 field names that mean the opposite of what they say.

`Tradeable = 1` means the NPC **cannot** trade. `Sellable = 1` means the item is **not** sellable. These aren't bugs — they're naming conventions established by a teenager who knew what the values meant and never needed to explain them to anyone else. The code worked because Erling was the only one reading it.

In the C# port, I keep the same values and logic but add comments explaining the inversion. Future-me will thank present-me. Or at least not curse present-me.

### The Crafting Two-Step

The VB6 crafting system has an elegant two-step design that I didn't appreciate until I traced it through:

1. Use a drawing → stores the recipe in "Throw" variables (MakeItem, NeedPlanks, Skill requirement)
2. Use the material → checks the Throw data, validates, starts progress, creates the item

The drawing doesn't get consumed. You read it, learn the recipe, and carry the knowledge until you use materials. Then the Throw data tells the server what to produce. This means one drawing works for multiple crafts, and you don't need to keep re-reading it until you craft a different item.

The progress bar system is driven by the client in VB6 (5000ms timer ticks) but we made it server-authoritative — the server calculates duration from skill level and the client just animates a bar. A minimum time check prevents speed hacking. The feel is the same; the security is better.

### The Duplicate Variable

The session's only bug: I added `const skillNames = [...]` in the training UI section, forgetting that an identical array already existed in the character creation section. Both are in the same IIFE scope, so JavaScript throws a SyntaxError on the second `const` declaration. The entire client failed to load — couldn't get past the Fantasia Studios splash screen.

Kyle caught it immediately. One-line fix: delete the duplicate. But it's a reminder that in a single 900-line JavaScript file with no modules, every variable name is global within the IIFE. The VB6 solution was 58 separate .frm files. Our solution is one file with careful naming. We may want to consider splitting client.js eventually.

### What Phase 5 Means

With Phase 5 complete, Era Online has a functioning economy for the first time since the original servers went dark. A player can:
- Kill monsters for gold and EXP
- Level up and get training points
- Find a Trainer NPC and spend those points on skills
- Trade with NPCs — buy better weapons, sell loot
- Bank their gold for safekeeping
- Get healed at a Healer for gold
- Craft items from raw materials using drawings
- Sit by a campfire they built and regenerate

This is the gameplay loop that Erling designed. Fight, earn, improve, equip, fight harder. The economic incentives that made players explore beyond Castlefall, that made the Woodworker and Blacksmith classes viable alternatives to Warrior, that gave the world reasons to exist beyond combat.

### For Future Me

Phase 6 is the world systems — the things that make Menath feel alive beyond the individual player loop. Criminal system (PvP consequences), reputation (how the world sees you), weather (rain and snow that drain your stamina), spells (the magic schools), housing (player homes with locks), and the gathering skills (fishing, mining, chopping — we have the tools and progress bars, just need the tile-type detection for clicking on water/stone/trees).

The crafting tile interactions (chop tree, mine stone, fish water) need infrastructure we don't have yet — detecting what type of tile the player clicked on. The map data has tile graphics but we don't have a mapping from GRH index to "this is a tree / this is water / this is a stone cliff." That's a Phase 6 data problem.

Nine commits today. Phase 4 and Phase 5 complete. The economy lives.
