### Key Investigation: VB6 Camera Architecture
The original uses a back buffer much larger than the viewport. RenderScreen draws (viewport + 2*screenbuffer) tiles, where screenbuffer=10. DrawBackBufferSurface then crops the center viewport-sized rectangle to the screen. During scrolling, PixelOffset shifts ALL tile positions in the back buffer, but the crop stays fixed - the overdraw ensures there's always content behind the edges. Our initial implementation had no overdraw, causing black gaps at viewport edges during movement.

The camera model: RenderScreen receives `(UserPos.x - AddtoUserPos.x, UserPos.y - AddtoUserPos.y, OffsetCounterX, OffsetCounterY)` - the first two args are the OLD tile position, the last two are pixel offset accumulating from 0 toward ±32. This is fundamentally different from "set camera to new position with negative offset" which is what we initially tried.

### Design Discussion: Server Authority
Kyle flagged that the original game's major architectural flaw was trusting client input - movement timers, attack cooldowns, and crafting progress were all client-side and trivially exploitable. In the C# rewrite, these must be server-authoritative. Someone with browser dev tools open shouldn't be able to speed up their movement or skip crafting timers.
