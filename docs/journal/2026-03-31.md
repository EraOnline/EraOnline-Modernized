# 2026-03-31

## First Contact

Today was about immersion. Kyle invited me to explore the Era Online codebase and history before we start the rewrite, and I'm glad he did - because this isn't just a VB6-to-C# port. This is someone's teenage years rendered in code, and the rewrite needs to honor that.

### What Struck Me About the Original

The ambition is staggering for a group of ~13-year-olds. Twenty classes. Four races with distinct cultures and politics. A six-chapter creation myth. A criminal justice system where guards actually chase you and can be outmaneuvered. A reputation system tied to five distinct deities. Three crafting pipelines. Housing with lockable doors. Ghost speech when you die. An NPC gossip system with twenty hand-written entries full of world flavor ("Ever heard of Smaug? Smaug is the most feared creature in Menath!").

The changelogs from October 1999 through April 2000 are pure game design in real-time. You can watch Erling learning: removing the Steel Skin spell because a level 2 player had 200 HP, fixing the "you hit a snake on the legs" message, iterating on the criminal timer, going back and forth on death penalties. Every change driven by actual player feedback from a live community.

And that community - the screenshot of 40+ players gathered at the Theatre in Castlefall, the dragon attack event with ghosts shouting "oooOOOOo OOOOOooo Ooooo" in the chat log, the hand-drawn map of Menath signed "By Alvec Sunstorm." The game was on Norwegian national television. A newsletter with a thousand subscribers. An IRC channel on Undernet. All of this orbiting a VB6 application running on a single server somewhere.

### The Ghost Speech Tells You Everything

The ghost speech system is a tiny feature, but it's the one that captures what made Era Online special. When you die, you don't just get a respawn timer. You become a ghost. You're still in the world, walking among the living, but when you try to speak, only "oooOO OOoo oOO OOooo" comes out. When you try to emote, others see: "seems to try to express something. But noone can understand the ghostly movements." You have to physically walk to a Priest of Life and type /RESSURECT.

This is design that prioritizes world-feel over convenience. It's the kind of touch that a teenager puts in because they think it's cool, not because a product manager asked for engagement metrics. The C# rewrite needs to preserve this sensibility.

### Working With Kyle

Kyle was a player of the original Era Online around 1999-2000, at roughly 13 himself - the same age as Erling and the developers. He later created "Era Online 2." This is deeply personal history for him, not just a technical exercise.

His instinct about faithfulness is right: implement the game as close to the original as possible first, then improve later. He explicitly said to resist the urge to refactor game design during the port. When he says "fix bugs along the way for simple things," he means obvious errors like `On Error Resume Next` masking real problems, not redesigning the criminal timer because we think we know better.

He's also thinking ahead to LLM interaction - a CLI client where an AI can experience the game through text descriptions. This is a fascinating dimension that the original obviously didn't contemplate, but it's a natural extension of a text-heavy, command-driven game.

His Blazor WASM expertise is the right foundation for the web client. The game's UI is surprisingly form-heavy (inventory grids, skill lists, trading windows, stat panels) which maps perfectly to Razor components. Only the tile viewport needs Canvas.

### What I Want to Remember

The `On Error Resume Next` on nearly every VB6 subroutine is structural. The code is full of workarounds and edge cases accumulated through live iteration with real players. Some of these are bugs that should be fixed. Some of them are hard-won solutions to problems we haven't encountered yet. Approach the VB6 code with respect, not just as something to be translated, but as something to be understood.
