# 2026-04-09

## Menath Gets Dangerous, Then Magical

Two big systems today: PvP combat and spells. The criminal system came first because everything else builds on the social consequence layer — guards already chase criminals from Phase 4, reputation data already flows, but the actual PvP loop and the criminal flag enforcement didn't exist.

### The PvP Moment

Kyle tested it by opening two browser tabs, walking both characters north through the Castlefall gate to Map 142 (the Outskirts — PK-allowed), and having one attack the other. The chat log told the story: "You are attacking a innocent ! You criminal !" in red, then the strike messages, then a ghost. The full social consequence chain working: attack innocent → criminal flag → reputation loss with nobles → reputation gain with Bendarr and the underworld.

The map lookup to find PK-allowed zones was a small adventure. Castlefall (81) and its two adjacent interior maps (79, 80) are all PK-free. There are no edge exits — the city is entirely connected by tile exits (doors, gates). Map 142 is the first PK-allowed zone, reached through the north gate tiles at row 17. I should have known this from reading the map data during the annotation pass, but I hadn't thought to check pkFreeZone flags until Kyle asked "what's the nearest PK zone?"

### The DI Lesson

The only runtime error today was a dependency injection issue: I added `GameLoopService` to GameHub's constructor so it could call `UserDieFromHub` for PvP kills, but `AddHostedService` doesn't register the type for injection — it creates its own instance internally. The fix is the standard pattern: register as singleton first, then pass the singleton to AddHostedService. I should have caught this before pushing — it's a well-known ASP.NET Core gotcha. Kyle had to report it from runtime logs.

### Spells as Data

The spell system was satisfying to implement because the VB6 design is fundamentally data-driven. Each spell is a bag of effect flags (damageHp, healHp, giveFood, teleport, resurrection, etc.) and the cast functions just iterate through them. CastSpellAtPC and CastSpellAtNPC are 200+ lines each in VB6, but most of that is the same pattern repeated: `If Spell.GiveHp > 0 Then` apply the effect. The C# port is more compact but preserves the same structure.

The school system is elegant in its simplicity. Each spell lists which schools can cast it (Nature, Destruction, Enchanting). Your class determines your school. That's the entire magic restriction system — no skill checks, no level requirements, just school membership. A level 1 Wizard with 300 mana can cast Hellfire (70 damage, 300 mana cost) if they can get the scroll.

What I found charming: all 16 spells have their own identity tied to the five deities. "Healing Hand of Veega" (goddess of birth and death), "Striking Hand of Bendarr" (god of war), "Fortune of Zeendic" (god of commerce gives you gold), "Illusion of Griigo" (god of logic makes you invisible), "Experience of Griigo" (god of wisdom grants knowledge). The spell names ARE the world-building.

### The Map Name Box

A small UI issue turned into a three-commit back-and-forth. A white box labeled with the map name was overlapping the Spells button on the right side of the chrome. I hid it with `display: none`, Kyle noticed the status bar text at the bottom disappeared too (it didn't — it just doesn't populate until first movement), we reverted, un-reverted, then just deleted the element entirely. The lesson: when Kyle reports a visual issue, get a screenshot and understand exactly what he's seeing before changing things.

### For Future Me

Phase 6 is progressing well. Criminal system and spells are the two biggest systems and they're done. What remains is mostly small individually — gathering skills (fish/mine/chop need tile-type detection), world flavor (bandage, roast meat, gossip, evaluate, eat/drink timer), animal taming, weather, housing, and ambient sounds. The gathering skills share infrastructure so they should go together. Everything else is 1-2 functions each.

The spell system has a few effect handlers that are referenced in the data but not yet wired up: invisibility (body change to invisible), paralyze (freeze target), summon creature (spawn NPC). These could be added later when needed — the flag checks are in the code, they just don't have visual/mechanical effects yet.
